# Raw Water Impeller Replacement Guide

## What is the Impeller?

The raw water impeller is a **flexible rubber propeller** inside the raw water pump. It pushes seawater through the cooling system. When it fails, the engine overheats within minutes.

**Replace every 200–300 hours OR every 2 seasons — whichever comes first.**

---

## Tools Needed
- Flathead screwdriver (large) or impeller puller tool
- Correct replacement impeller (brand + model specific)
- New pump cover gasket (order as set)
- Petroleum jelly or dish soap (lubricate impeller)
- Container to catch water
- Rags

---

## Universal Replacement Procedure

### Step 1: Preparation
1. Close the raw water seacock (through-hull valve)
2. Run engine briefly (10 seconds) to drain remaining water, then STOP
3. Have rags ready — residual water will spill

### Step 2: Remove Pump Cover
1. Locate raw water pump — on front or side of engine (follow the raw water hose from sea strainer)
2. Remove 4–6 bolts holding the cover plate
3. Pull cover off — water will drain
4. Note the direction the cover comes off (remember which way impeller was turning)

### Step 3: Remove Old Impeller
1. Grip impeller with both thumbs and pull — it's press-fit onto the shaft
2. If stuck: insert two flathead screwdrivers either side and lever gently
3. Professional tip: use an impeller puller tool (cheap, prevents damage)
4. **CHECK for missing vanes!** — If any vanes broke off, they are lodged downstream in the cooling circuit (heat exchanger, thermostat housing). You MUST find all pieces or they will block cooling flow.

### Step 4: Find Missing Vanes
- Inspect in heat exchanger inlet
- Inspect in thermostat housing
- Inspect where raw water exits into exhaust (muffler/waterlift)
- If you can't find all pieces, flush system with hose before starting

### Step 5: Install New Impeller
1. Apply thin coat of petroleum jelly or dish soap to impeller and bore
2. Insert impeller: bend vanes in rotation direction (same as old one) and push in
3. Fit new cover gasket (dry — no sealant needed)
4. Replace cover, torque bolts finger-tight + 1/4 turn (don't overtighten — it's aluminium)

### Step 6: Test
1. Open seacock
2. Start engine
3. Immediately check tell-tale (pišalica) for water flow
4. Watch temperature gauge — should remain normal within 2 minutes
5. Check for leaks around pump cover

## Outboard Water Pump Replacement (Different from Inboard)

Outboard water pumps are located **in the lower unit (leg)**, not easily accessible:
1. Remove lower unit (gearcase) — requires removing prop and several bolts
2. Pump housing is inside gearcase
3. Replace impeller + housing seal as a kit
4. This is a 1–2 hour job at a marina, manageable DIY if mechanically confident

**Symptoms of failed outboard impeller:** No tell-tale stream, or very weak stream. Engine overheats. Stop immediately.
