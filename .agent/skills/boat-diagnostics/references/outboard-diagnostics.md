# Outboard Engine Diagnostics — Complete Guide

## Quick Fault → Cause Reference Table

| Symptom | Most Likely Cause | Quick Check |
|---|---|---|
| Won't crank | Kill switch not connected, dead battery | Reconnect lanyard; check battery |
| Cranks, won't fire | Flooded, no fuel, spark issue | Wait 5 min; check fuel bulb; check plug |
| Starts, dies immediately | Kill switch intermittent, fuel flow blocked | Check lanyard; check fuel cock |
| Runs rough at idle | Dirty carburettor, old fuel, fouled plug | Clean carb idle jet; fresh fuel |
| OK at idle, dies at WOT | Fuel starvation (vent, pump, filter) | Open tank vent; check fuel flow |
| Loss of power | Fouled prop, cavitation, worn powerhead | Inspect prop; check trim |
| Overheating | Impeller, blocked water inlet, thermostat | Check tell-tale; check strainer |
| Loud knock / clunk | Prop strike, lower unit damage | Stop immediately; inspect prop and gearcase |
| Oil alarm | Low oil (4-stroke), wrong mix (2-stroke) | Check oil level; check mix ratio |
| Excessive vibration | Damaged prop blade, loose mounting | Inspect prop; check mounting bolts |
| White smoke | Mixing exhaust + water = normal. Excess = head gasket | If constant with bad smell → mechanic |
| Blue smoke | Oil burning (2-stroke excess oil, or 4-stroke issue) | Reduce oil mix (2T) or engine service |
| Black smoke | Rich fuel mixture, dirty air filter, choke stuck | Check choke, clean air filter |
| Surging / hunting | Idle mixture screw, dirty pilot jet | Carburettor clean or idle adjust |

## Yamaha Outboard — Specific Notes

### Self-Diagnosis (Yamaha 4-Stroke F series)
- **Error flash codes:** Count flashes on warning light on tachometer
  - 1 flash: Oil pressure
  - 2 flashes: Overheat
  - 3 flashes: Tilt sensor
  - 8 flashes: Shift position (common — disengage and re-engage gear)

### Common Yamaha Issues
| Model | Known Issue | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| F40/F50/F60 | VST (vapour separator) fuel starvation | Clean/replace VST filter |
| F115/F150 | TPS (throttle position sensor) fault | Reset or replace TPS |
| 2-stroke 50–90 HP | Reed valve wear at high hours | Inspect reeds; replace if cracked |

## Mercury / Mariner — Specific Notes

### SmartCraft Display Codes (Mercury 4-stroke)
- **E0:** No fault
- **EA:** Overheat warning
- **E4:** Low oil pressure
- Common: Temperature sensor fault gives false overheat — test sensor in hot water before replacing engine

### Mercury EFI (Electronic Fuel Injection) Issues
- **Won't start cold:** IAC (idle air controller) sticky — spray carb cleaner, cycle ignition 5x
- **Hesitation at WOT:** MAF sensor dirty — clean with MAF cleaner spray

## Johnson / Evinrude 2-Stroke — Specific Notes

*(Production ended 2020 but many still in use)*

- **VRO Oil Injection (Variable Ratio Oiling):** Check VRO pump operation — if it fails, engine runs lean and seizes
- **Test VRO:** Listen for clicking sound from pump while running. No click = pump problem
- **Fuel primer bulb won't get hard:** Check anti-siphon valve in tank fitting — very common issue
- **Power pack (CDI) failure:** Common on older engines — engine fires on some cylinders only

## Carburettor Cleaning (For Carb Outboards)

Tools: carb cleaner spray, compressed air, small flathead screwdriver, lint-free cloth

1. Remove air filter cover and air filter
2. Locate idle mixture screw (usually under a cap — some are sealed with a plug)
3. Remove main jet and pilot jet — tiny brass screws with holes in them
4. Spray carb cleaner through all passages — you should see daylight
5. Use compressed air to blow through all jets
6. Reinstall jets firmly (don't overtighten — they're soft brass)
7. Set idle mixture: turn screw in (clockwise) until snug, then back out 1.5–2.5 turns as starting point
8. Adjust idle RPM with idle speed screw for smooth 700–800 RPM tickover

## Spark Plug Reference

| Symptom | Plug Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Engine OK | Light tan/grey electrode | Correct mixture |
| Rich mixture | Black, sooty deposit | Too much fuel / choke stuck on |
| Lean mixture | White, chalky electrode | Too little fuel — possible damage |
| Oil fouled | Black, oily | Oil getting into combustion |
| Overheated | Melted electrode | Severe overheating — stop! |

**Check gap:** Use feeler gauge. Typical outboard gap: 0.5–1.0mm (check your manual).

## Spare Parts to Always Carry (Outboard)

- [ ] Spare spark plugs (one per cylinder)
- [ ] Spare water pump impeller kit (with housing and seal)
- [ ] Spare fuel filter
- [ ] Spare fuel primer bulb and fuel hose fitting
- [ ] Spare shear pin (or prop hub, if applicable)
- [ ] Outboard oil (correct grade for your engine)
- [ ] Spare propeller (or at minimum a folding prop wrench)
- [ ] Kill switch lanyard — spare (they break)
- [ ] Fuses (all sizes used by engine)
